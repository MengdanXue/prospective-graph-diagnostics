\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Observed Portfolio Dependence and a Possible Mismatch}
\label{sec:mismatch}

The frozen full-portfolio comparison gives a negative result for the evaluated diagnostic, while the post-hoc restrictions show that its relative regret changes with the graph action. Combined improves on always-graph in descriptive mean regret for GCN and GAT, but performs worse for the other four individual architectures. These observations motivate studying the pairing between a diagnostic and its candidate models. They do not show that diagnostic utility is determined solely by an architecture's treatment of heterophily.

Table~\ref{tab:winning_architectures} describes the composition of the graph action. H2GCN, LINKX, and GPR-GNN are validation-selected within the graph portfolio in 95 of the 110 units, including all ten units on PubMed and all ten on Coauthor-CS. This is an architecture-selection count; it is not the number of units in which graph beats MLP. In particular, the selected graph model can still lose to MLP or fall short of the practical margin.

The four datasets carrying 95.1\% of Combined's full-set regret---Roman-empire, Squirrel, Amazon-ratings, and Chameleon---all lie below its frozen homophily threshold. The rule declines the graph action on all 40 units, and a heterophily-aware architecture is validation-selected within the graph portfolio on 35 of them. Their mean graph-minus-MLP gaps range from 15.9 to 23.7 percentage points. The statistic's threshold therefore excludes the graph action exactly where this particular rule incurs most of its observed cost.

This is a descriptive mismatch for the frozen rule, not a proof that no scalar threshold can improve it. Lowering a threshold changes both costly exclusions and unnecessary graph selections, and the balance depends on the metric and portfolio. Non-monotone rules, alternative thresholds, additional features, or learned selectors remain untested here. Likewise, the restriction results do not identify a monotone architecture-category ordering: GPR-GNN gives lower absolute Combined regret than GCN and GAT, even though Combined does not beat always-graph for GPR-GNN. The mechanism behind the observed variation remains a hypothesis to test with independent evidence.

\subsection{What the Decision Evaluation Establishes}

The main benchmark evaluates diagnostics as actions, with a fixed practical margin, common MLP fallback, and identical units across policies. The combined rule incurs greater descriptive regret than always-graph and fails the predeclared requirement for an incremental-value claim. This result is bounded to the evaluated statistics, thresholds, datasets, candidate architectures, and training grid.

Graph is the margin-defined target in 89 of 110 units. That imbalance makes always-graph a strong reference and demonstrates why raw selection accuracy alone is insufficient. Regret measures the cost of a wrong action, and coverage exposes the consequences of abstention. The portfolio restrictions then show why a policy must be compared with the relevant reference under each candidate set: neither the reference's performance nor the diagnostic's value transfers automatically.

The dataset-level paired analysis does not establish confirmatory superiority after the frozen Holm correction. Its observed resolution limit is not an equivalence result or a blanket statement of zero power for every possible dataset outcome. The post-hoc architecture analysis also does not imply that richer diagnostics must fail. Future proposals should report uncertainty and evaluate their decisions against an explicitly specified portfolio on data not used to develop the selector.

\subsection{Graph Signal and Diagnostic Value}

Degree-preserving randomization produces large paired GCN losses on Cora, CiteSeer, and PubMed. Useful non-degree edge organization is therefore present in those settings, even though the evaluated diagnostics are unreliable selectors across the benchmark. The intervention changes several structural attributes simultaneously, so it cannot identify homophily as the cause or establish a universal mechanism. The fixed-degree calculation in Appendix~\ref{sec:snr} provides scoped context for interactions among label mixing, degree, and feature strength; it is not a graph-model-selection theorem.

Validation selection has the lowest descriptive regret in the full benchmark but requires training both model families. It is a performance reference, not a cost-free diagnostic or a guaranteed upper bound. The combined rule also uses MLP validation accuracy, so only its label-dependent graph statistics are training-label-only. Claims about cheap selection must account for the information and training already available at decision time.

\subsection{Limitations and Next Tests}

The study covers 11 public node-classification datasets, ten seeds, one stratified split design, seven architectures, four trials per architecture, and test accuracy. Dataset-level inference resamples 11 dataset clusters; the 110 seed-level units are not treated as independent datasets. The exploratory-origin thresholds were fixed before the confirmatory records but after legacy aggregates had been inspected. The public snapshot provides file identities and execution provenance, while the earlier private history is not independently accessible.

The original graph action receives 24 trials against the MLP's four. The post-hoc restrictions match trial counts only and alter the candidate architecture as well as the search space. They do not test intermediate portfolios, a 24-trial MLP grid, equal training time or FLOPs, or an independent dataset set. All restrictions reuse outcomes from the original benchmark, and the architecture-group interpretation was developed afterward. These limits prevent a claim of stable external performance or a causal decomposition of portfolio, architecture, and compute effects.

The next useful test is an independent prospective evaluation of a specified selector against specified candidate portfolios. Such a study should plan dataset-level precision and multiplicity, distinguish trial counts from resource consumption, and keep threshold development separate from final evaluation. The present findings and release provide a basis for that test.
