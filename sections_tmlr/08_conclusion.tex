\section{Conclusion}

Under a frozen 11-dataset graph-versus-MLP decision protocol, the combined diagnostic incurs greater descriptive regret than always-graph and does not establish incremental value. An explicitly post-hoc analysis shows that the unchanged rule has lower mean regret than always-graph when the graph action is GCN or GAT, but higher regret for LINKX, GPR-GNN, GraphSAGE, and H2GCN. This is evidence of observed portfolio dependence at matched trial count, not equal compute or stable generalization beyond the studied datasets.

The architecture-selection and regret-concentration summaries suggest a mismatch between the frozen threshold and the full heterophily-aware portfolio. They do not establish that every scalar homophily rule must fail. The separate degree-preserving intervention shows that diagnostic failure can coexist with useful edge organization in the tested settings. We release the frozen artifacts and decision-evaluation protocol so that alternative diagnostics and candidate portfolios can be assessed with explicit information boundaries, reference policies, regret, coverage, and dataset-level uncertainty.
