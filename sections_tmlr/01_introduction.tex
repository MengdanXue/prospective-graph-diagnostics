\section{Introduction}

Graph structure can provide information beyond node features, but its value depends on the model and task~\cite{kipf2017gcn,velivckovic2018gat,hamilton2017graphsage}. A practical question is whether node classification should use a tuned graph-model portfolio or a tuned feature-only multilayer perceptron (MLP). A diagnostic that can avoid an unhelpful graph-model search would be useful, provided that its decisions are evaluated against the actual candidate models and training budget.

Existing work already shows that homophily alone is insufficient to characterize GNN performance and develops class-conditional, feature, structural, and neighborhood-distribution metrics~\cite{luan2023whendo,platonov2023characterizing,zheng2024trihom,pmlr-v235-wang24u}. Learned selectors also rank graph models across datasets~\cite{park2023metagl,park2023glemos}. Our question is narrower: how do fixed transparent graph/MLP/abstain policies perform against trivial and validation-based policies, and how does their measured value change when the graph action refers to a different candidate portfolio?

We first evaluate a frozen protocol on newly generated records. It fixes datasets, seeds, splits, architectures, trial grids, label scope, thresholds, abstention fallback, a one-percentage-point practical margin, and dataset-level inference. Legacy aggregate outcomes had been inspected during exploratory work; the exact combined threshold pair was frozen subsequently, before the confirmatory records. An outcome-independent eligibility amendment excluded Texas because a singleton class cannot support the required three-way split and restarted the remaining datasets. The final artifact contains 11 datasets, 110 dataset--seed units, 770 selected-model records, and no failed final-run units. The graph action selects among six architectures with 24 trials in total; the MLP action receives four trials. This is an asymmetric operational comparison.

Under that comparison, the combined rule attains 55.5\% selection accuracy at 68.2\% coverage and incurs 7.46 percentage points of full-set regret. Always-graph attains 80.9\% accuracy and 0.26 points, while validation selection attains 91.8\% and 0.22 points. The result is a failure of the evaluated rule to improve on the reference policy in this benchmark, rather than a general impossibility result for graph diagnostics.

\paragraph{The observed value changes with the candidate portfolio.}
A post-hoc sensitivity analysis restricts the graph action to each individual architecture without retraining models or changing the diagnostic's actions. Each restricted action receives four trials, matching the MLP in trial count rather than total compute. The combined rule has lower descriptive mean regret than always-graph for GCN (1.74 versus 5.29 points) and GAT (2.08 versus 6.35), but higher regret for LINKX (7.99 versus 2.13). Always-graph also has lower regret for GPR-GNN, GraphSAGE, and H2GCN. The same policy can therefore improve or worsen observed regret relative to the trivial reference as the candidate portfolio changes. These are descriptive findings on the original datasets, not a new prospective validation or evidence of a stable advantage on unseen datasets.

\paragraph{Architecture selection helps locate, rather than prove, a mismatch.}
H2GCN, LINKX, and GPR-GNN are validation-selected within the graph portfolio in 95 of the 110 units, including every unit on PubMed and Coauthor-CS. This count describes graph-architecture selection, not 95 graph-versus-MLP victories. Four datasets account for 95.1\% of the combined rule's regret; their training-label homophily lies below the frozen threshold and the rule declines the graph action on all 40 units. This concentration is consistent with a mismatch between the fixed threshold and the portfolio it governs. It neither identifies a universal mechanism nor rules out alternative thresholds, non-monotone rules, richer diagnostics, or learned selectors.

The paper offers three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{An empirical study of portfolio-dependent diagnostic value.} A frozen decision benchmark and explicitly post-hoc single-architecture comparisons show how the relative regret of an unchanged rule varies with the graph action. We report all six restrictions, including results that favor the diagnostic over always-graph.
    \item \textbf{A reproducible decision-evaluation protocol.} The release connects training-label scope, validation-based selection, nine diagnostic and reference policies, abstention, oracle-portfolio regret, dataset-level inference, and immutable provenance. It enables direct checks of decisions and outcomes, beyond performance correlations.
    \item \textbf{Evidence separating graph signal from diagnostic value.} A degree-preserving intervention reduces GCN test accuracy by 45.5, 29.9, and 17.3 points on Cora, CiteSeer, and PubMed. This shows sensitivity to non-degree edge organization in those settings, without identifying homophily as the causal attribute.
\end{enumerate}

The original frozen comparison remains the confirmatory analysis. Portfolio restrictions and architecture-selection summaries are exploratory analyses of the same records; they motivate validation on independent datasets rather than replacing it.
