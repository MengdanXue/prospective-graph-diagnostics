## 1. Contribution beyond failure of a weak threshold

**Anticipated concern:** The negative result may only show that an arbitrary heuristic is weak, while prior work already questions homophily.

**Draft response:** We agree that failure of the frozen rule does not establish a limitation of all graph diagnostics. Our contribution is a bounded evaluation of a specified operational decision: whether a transparent policy should choose the validation-selected graph portfolio or the validation-selected MLP. The protocol makes the candidate models, decision-time information, practical margin, abstention fallback, regret, coverage, and reference policies explicit. Combined incurs 7.46 percentage points of full-set regret, compared with 0.26 for always-graph and 0.22 for validation selection. The additional post-hoc analysis reports how the unchanged rule's relative regret changes when the graph action changes, rather than presenting that rule as a new strong selector. Its mean regret is lower than always-graph for GCN and GAT restrictions, but higher for the other four single architectures. We retain both favorable and unfavorable comparisons and make no claim to be the first study of homophily limitations, graph utility, or graph model selection. A representative head-to-head evaluation of published diagnostic methods remains outside the present evidence.

**Evidence and manuscript anchors:** Claim rows D1 and D5; [introduction](../sections_tmlr/01_introduction.tex); [portfolio sensitivity](../sections_tmlr/04a_equal_budget_sensitivity.tex); existing [novelty audit](novelty_audit_negative_diagnostic_study_2026-08-13.md). The novelty audit is a dated repository record, not a fresh or exhaustive literature search performed for this revision package.

## 2. Graph has six architectures and 24 trials; MLP has four trials

**Anticipated concern:** The graph action's larger search budget could make the comparison unfair or explain its strength.

**Draft response:** The comparison concerns fixed asymmetric operational portfolios: the graph action selects among six architectures with four trials each, while the MLP action has four trials for one architecture. We do not interpret it as an equal-compute comparison between model families. The post-hoc single-architecture restrictions match trial counts at four per action, but neither equalize time or FLOPs nor isolate the causal effect of compute, because they also change the available architecture and search space. At the same eight-trial graph budget, GAT+GCN gives Combined/always-graph regrets of 2.27/5.50 points, while H2GCN+LINKX gives 7.59/0.27. These observations show variation with architecture composition at a fixed trial count within the existing records. They do not replace a separately executed resource-matched study or a larger MLP tuning grid. Validation selection also requires training both actions and is a performance reference rather than a cost-free diagnostic.

**Evidence and manuscript anchors:** D2 and D5; [protocol](../sections_tmlr/03_decision_protocol.tex); [all-subset comparison](../sections_tmlr/04a_equal_budget_sensitivity.tex). No equal-time, equal-FLOPs, or 24-trial MLP experiment is claimed or promised here.

## 3. Combined is a single threshold after fallback

**Anticipated concern:** The name Combined may overstate the information used for its evaluated decisions.

**Draft response:** Under the complete inputs observed in this benchmark, the full-set policy simplifies exactly to graph if training-label homophily is at least 0.55 and MLP otherwise. Its 0.40 MLP-validation cutoff distinguishes explicit MLP decisions from abstentions, but the common MLP fallback makes those branches identical for full-set actions and regret. We therefore interpret Combined as the historical name of the pre-fallback rule, not as evidence that a richer deployed selector was evaluated. The exact threshold pair was fixed after earlier exploratory aggregate outcomes had been inspected and before generation of the confirmatory records; this is not whole-project preregistration or an independently developed diagnostic. Moreover, no observed homophily lies in the open interval (0.3848, 0.7125), so every threshold strictly inside that interval gives the same 110 actions. The benchmark provides little resolution for comparisons among those cutoffs.

**Evidence and manuscript anchors:** D3; [prospective specification](preregistration_diagnostic_benchmark.md); [protocol](../sections_tmlr/03_decision_protocol.tex); [prospective results](../sections_tmlr/04_prospective_results.tex). The conclusion must remain about the evaluated rules and thresholds, not all scalar statistics.

## 4. All 63 portfolios are post-hoc and the favorable cases depend on dataset composition

**Anticipated concern:** Portfolio-dependent value may be a selected or fragile observation.

**Draft response:** All portfolio restrictions were formulated after the frozen outcomes were available. We therefore report them as exploratory reanalyses and enumerate all 63 nonempty subsets, rather than selecting only favorable restrictions. Only GAT, GCN, and their pair have lower descriptive Combined regret than always-graph. The advantages are sensitive to dataset composition: removing Cornell and Wisconsin together makes Combined-minus-always-graph regret +1.11 points for GAT and +0.92 for GCN, with no advantage in any of the 63 subsets. We report this dependence as a limitation, not as a reason to omit these datasets. The subsets share models and dataset outcomes, so their counts are not independent replications or a probability of future success. Our claim is only that relative regret varies with the candidate portfolio in these observations; the evidence does not establish a stable out-of-dataset advantage.

**Evidence and manuscript anchors:** D5--D6; [portfolio_robustness.json](../results/diagnostic/route_a_prospective_v2/analysis/portfolio_robustness.json); [sensitivity section](../sections_tmlr/04a_equal_budget_sensitivity.tex). Each row uses its own graph/MLP oracle. GPR-GNN's absolute Combined regret is 1.50 points, below GCN's 1.74 and GAT's 2.08; absolute regret is not the same as improvement over always-graph.

## 5. A calibrated threshold may perform better

**Anticipated concern:** The frozen cutoff does not adequately test whether homophily contains useful decision information.

**Draft response:** We agree with that distinction. A post-hoc leave-one-dataset-out control chooses a homophily threshold using only the other ten datasets' outcomes in each fold. For the full portfolio it lowers mean regret from 7.46 to 2.41 points, although always-graph remains lower at 0.26 points. Thus failure of the frozen threshold is not evidence that every threshold must fail. The training datasets' selected-model test outcomes serve explicitly as meta-training targets; the held-out dataset does not select its threshold. Nevertheless, the control was designed after the original study was inspected, its training folds overlap, and graph domains may be related. We do not present it as an independent prospective evaluation or as a replacement for representative published selectors.

**Evidence and manuscript anchors:** D7; [portfolio_robustness.json](../results/diagnostic/route_a_prospective_v2/analysis/portfolio_robustness.json); [calibration subsection](../sections_tmlr/04a_equal_budget_sensitivity.tex). No new confirmatory p-value is attached to these comparisons.

## 6. Raw-feature sensitivity covers only two selected datasets

**Anticipated concern:** NormalizeFeatures may depress the MLP, while two-dataset sensitivity cannot validate the whole benchmark.

**Draft response:** We acknowledge both points. The post-hoc experiment retrains all seven architectures under raw features and PyG 2.7.0 NormalizeFeatures on the same ten splits of Roman-empire and Amazon-ratings; it does not combine a new MLP with an old graph result. Raw features increase mean MLP and selected-graph test accuracy by 49.48 and 38.81 points on Roman-empire, and by 4.59 and 0.11 points on Amazon-ratings. The graph action remains the margin-defined target in all ten seeds on each dataset under both conditions. With raw features, the graph-minus-MLP gaps are 14.77 and 11.82 points, and the frozen rule still chooses MLP. The experiment therefore shows that preprocessing materially changes the estimand and the cost of the rule's decisions on these two datasets, without reversing their target action. Because these graphs were chosen after a concern about their continuous features, this is a restricted sensitivity analysis, not independent validation of the 11-dataset result or evidence that normalization alone explains that result.

**Evidence and manuscript anchors:** P1--P2; [plan](preprocessing_sensitivity_plan.md); [summary](../results/diagnostic/route_a_prospective_v2/analysis/preprocessing_sensitivity.json); [sensitivity table](../sections_tmlr/04a_equal_budget_sensitivity.tex). Excluding the original Chameleon and Squirrel versions is separately documented; it does not replace retraining on duplicate-filtered graphs.

## 7. Low normalized performance may be a training problem

**Anticipated concern:** Apparent model or graph weaknesses could reflect input parameterization and the finite optimization recipe.

**Draft response:** The development diagnostics support this concern and limit our interpretation of architecture performance. A validation-only MLP experiment crosses five feature transformations with two weight-decay settings, producing 200 selected records and 800 trials with zero new test evaluations. At weight decay 0.0005, train-feature-fitted centering and global scaling of the normalized input raises mean validation accuracy from 16.75% to 66.45% on Roman-empire and from 36.80% to 42.65% on Amazon-ratings. Prediction counts directly show majority-class constant predictions in all ten Amazon-ratings seeds under the original normalized input; removing weight decay alone does not eliminate them. The affine transformation preserves feature information up to floating-point effects and can be absorbed by the MLP's biased input layer at evaluation, supporting sensitivity of the training recipe rather than newly supplied information. It does not identify a unique optimizer mechanism or establish test generalization. The subsequent graph-model transfer run remains a development analysis with disclosed numerical variability; the MLP argument cannot automatically be applied to GCN or GAT because graph aggregation and attention interact with centering.

**Evidence and manuscript anchors:** P3--P5; [MLP result interpretation](mlp_optimization_diagnostic_review.md); [MLP summary](../results/diagnostic/posthoc_mlp_optimization_v1/analysis/mlp_optimization_diagnostic_summary.json); [architecture-specific analysis](graph_parameterization_architecture_notes.md). The new graph-versus-MLP validation comparison selects and reports on the same validation set and is selection-optimistic. No new held-out target actions or regrets are inferred from it.

## 8. Statistical non-significance is not equivalence

**Anticipated concern:** Large regret differences coexist with non-significant corrected tests, and the inferential sample is small.

**Draft response:** We distinguish descriptive performance from the confirmatory decision rule. Always-graph minus Combined mean regret is -7.20 points, with an unadjusted bootstrap interval of [-13.17, -1.70] and raw sign-flip p = 0.015625; its Holm-adjusted p-value is 0.125 across the eight predeclared comparisons. Consequently, we do not claim corrected superiority for always-graph, nor treat non-significance as equivalence. The narrower incremental-value conclusion follows from the diagnostic's failure to show improvement under the stated protocol, rather than from proving a null or an opposite superiority claim. Seeds are averaged within datasets before inference. The exact sign-flip reference distribution requires joint invariance to the enumerated sign changes; a zero mean or paired observations alone do not establish this condition. The eleven convenience-selected datasets also do not automatically represent a population of graphs. Seven nonzero differences in one direction impose the observed raw p-value floor of 0.015625; this does not mean the design had zero power under every possible outcome.

**Evidence and manuscript anchors:** D4; [prospective results](../sections_tmlr/04_prospective_results.tex); [inference assumptions](../sections_tmlr/03_decision_protocol.tex); [diagnostic audit](../results/diagnostic/route_a_prospective_v2/analysis/diagnostic_audit.json). Dataset-level uncertainty remains primary; 110 seed units are not 110 independent datasets.

## 9. LINKX variability and what a reliability audit can establish

**Anticipated concern:** Large control drift could undermine the interpretation of graph-model transfer results.

**Draft response:** We investigated the control drift through a separately fixed, validation-only audit comprising 22 independent training processes, all completed with zero new test evaluations. For Roman-empire/NormalizeFeatures/LINKX seed 4, three default-CUDA repetitions of trial_001 attain validation accuracies of 13.84%, 40.66%, and 13.86%; trial_003 gives 40.00%, 42.65%, and 28.32%. Their ranges are 26.82 and 14.33 percentage points. Initial parameter/buffer states and RNG hashes agree within each group, while logits, gradients, and post-step states already diverge at the first recorded training epoch. Repeating each trial three times under the fixed deterministic-CUDA configuration produces identical recorded histories and selected states within its group. This provides evidence of training-repeatability sensitivity to the tested backend settings, beyond mere record consistency checks.

**Draft response, continuation:** The audit also distinguishes training variation from evaluation of a fixed selected checkpoint. Repeated forward evaluation and reconstruction of the selected state produce zero prediction disagreements in the audited runs; the largest default-CUDA logit difference is approximately 1.53e-5, while deterministic runs have zero recorded logit differences. Under deterministic CUDA, LINKX seed 4/trial_001 gives 13.84% validation accuracy on normalized features, 60.09% on raw features, and 59.05% after centering and scaling, with identical repetitions. Thus the treatment direction persists for that fixed cell. Additional deterministic MLP and GAT controls repeat identically, as does a two-repeat CPU trace limited to 12 epochs. These are limited controls: the audit does not rerun the original four-trial selection across ten seeds, require CPU/GPU outcome equality, or establish a deterministic ranking for the whole model portfolio. The backend intervention bundles deterministic algorithms and CuBLAS/cuDNN/TF32 settings, while instrumentation itself can change scheduling. It therefore does not identify a universal kernel cause or prove all earlier control drift harmless. We retain the original frozen test outcomes and interpret the graph transfer results descriptively.

**Runtime boundary:** The original 770 records were trained on CUDA with an RTX 3070 Laptop GPU, PyTorch 2.9.1+cu126, Python 3.13.5, NumPy 2.2.6, and PyG 2.7.0. The audit uses the current RTX 5060/CUDA 12.8/Python 3.12 runtime. The original records do not preserve all deterministic-algorithm, cuDNN, and TF32 flags. Consequently, the new audit diagnoses repeatability of the tested local cells; it neither establishes that the original 770-record run had the same instability nor demonstrates repeatability in that original environment.

**Audit artifact and binding:** [audit_summary.json](../results/diagnostic/training_reproducibility_audit_v1/analysis/audit_summary.json) contains the configuration, manifest, worker records, and grouped outcomes. Its SHA-256 is `17af44a2535bfbcb6be62eb0b99ae9ae9a2b06c8ede7f4426805da30dad4475c`; the recorded configuration digest is `80657f2f48fd589568d353cf268f3ed3c59008b9468463779cc3c0008cec80ea`. The manifest records the source baseline, executable-file fingerprints, data binding, and worktree status; it must not be paraphrased as proof that every audit file was already committed at that baseline. ## 10. Degree-preserving randomization does not explain the main selector failures

**Anticipated concern:** The intervention uses different datasets and does not isolate homophily.

**Draft response:** The intervention is auxiliary evidence that diagnostic failure can coexist with useful edge organization. Degree-preserving randomization reduces GCN test accuracy by 45.5, 29.9, and 17.3 points on Cora, CiteSeer, and PubMed. It preserves degrees but changes multiple aspects of edge organization simultaneously; it does not identify homophily as the causal property. These citation graphs are distinct from the four datasets concentrating the selector's regret, and GCN is not necessarily their selected graph architecture. We therefore do not use the intervention to explain those failures. There are only three dataset clusters, with overall exact sign-flip p = 0.25; ten seeds per graph do not supply additional independent datasets. The accompanying fixed-degree calculation remains a scoped moment-based surrogate rather than a theorem about trained-GNN selection.

**Evidence and manuscript anchors:** [intervention summary](../results/diagnostic/route_a_degree_matched_v1/summary/summary.json); [intervention section](../sections_tmlr/05_edge_intervention.tex); [fixed-degree calculation](../sections/06_fixed_degree_analysis.tex).
