\title{From Graph Diagnostics to Model Decisions: Portfolio, Fallback, and Calibration}
\author*[1]{\fnm{Mengdan} \sur{Xue}}\email{17326961775@163.com}
\affil*[1]{\orgdiv{Faculty of Computational Mathematics and Cybernetics}, \orgname{Lomonosov Moscow State University}, \orgaddress{\city{Moscow}, \country{Russia}}}

\abstract{A graph diagnostic's value for model selection depends on the candidate models and calibration tasks. We audit graph-versus-multilayer-perceptron (MLP) policies on 11 node-classification datasets and ten seeds, measuring test accuracy lost relative to the better validation-selected candidate. A frozen homophily rule with MLP fallback incurs 7.46 percentage points of regret, compared with 0.26 for always-graph. Primary dataset-level tests establish no superiority after multiplicity correction. Post-hoc analyses vary inputs, MLP search budgets, portfolios, and calibration access. A training-label-only Gaussian Naive Bayes adaptation of the Classifier-based Performance Metric improves over both constant choices in all 12 restricted configurations using graph convolutional and graph attention models. It loses to always-graph in the four six-architecture configurations, where Roman-empire contributes 78.7--86.5\% of its regret. Omitting that dataset reverses three comparisons. Adjusted homophily achieves lower full-portfolio regret under leave-one-dataset-out calibration, but source-group holdout removes or reverses those gains. Jointly withholding Cornell and Wisconsin gives constant-graph decisions on both in every full-portfolio configuration. Probe scores also saturate, and some classes are absent from inner holdouts. Dataset-level accounting locates these conditional gains and losses, and executable reconstruction supports their verification. Diagnostic evaluation therefore needs explicit candidate portfolios and calibration-source boundaries.}
\keywords{Graph neural networks, Model selection, Graph diagnostics, Node classification, Benchmark evaluation, Decision regret}
