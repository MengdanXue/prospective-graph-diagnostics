\title{From Graph Diagnostics to Model Decisions: Portfolio, Fallback, and Calibration}
\author*[1]{\fnm{Mengdan} \sur{Xue}}\email{17326961775@163.com}
\affil*[1]{\orgdiv{Faculty of Computational Mathematics and Cybernetics}, \orgname{Lomonosov Moscow State University}, \orgaddress{\city{Moscow}, \country{Russia}}}

\abstract{Graph diagnostics guide model choices, so their utility depends on the candidate portfolio, calibration, and abstention fallback. We audit nine fixed graph-versus-multilayer-perceptron (MLP) policies on 11 node-classification datasets and ten seeds, measuring regret against the better validation-selected candidate. The frozen benchmark gives six graph architectures 24 tuning trials in total and MLP four trials. The historical Combined rule with MLP fallback incurs 7.46 percentage points of regret, compared with 0.26 for always-graph; four datasets contribute 95.1\% of its loss. Restricting the graph portfolio reverses some descriptive comparisons. Separate post-hoc retraining evaluates two input parameterizations and a 24-trial MLP search. Matched leave-one-dataset-out calibration preserves adjusted homophily's lower descriptive full-portfolio regret in all four input/budget configurations, with its centered-scaled, 24-trial advantage confined to Cornell and Wisconsin. A further 220-cell extension evaluates a training-label-only Gaussian Naive Bayes adaptation of the Classifier-based Performance Metric. Its fixed rule improves over always-graph for restricted GCN/GAT portfolios but incurs higher regret for the full portfolio in every configuration. The probe's affine invariance explains why input changes can leave its decisions unchanged while altering finite-budget neural training. Primary dataset-level tests establish no superiority after multiplicity correction. The resulting offline audit connects diagnostic scores to effective actions, reference headroom, and dataset-level benefit and harm, distinguishing saved-score reconstruction from new training and independent validation.}
\keywords{Graph neural networks, Model selection, Graph diagnostics, Node classification, Benchmark evaluation, Decision regret}

The accompanying \path{applied-intelligence-cpm-supplement-2026-10-08.zip} contains the current editable manuscript, the classifier-probe implementation and protocol, all 220 CPM metric records with their repeated inner-split accuracies, and complete fixed and calibrated policy outputs. Its \path{previous-revision.zip} preserves the preceding major-revision supplement byte for byte, including the matched-calibration and fallback/exclusion audit and its embedded integrated evidence archive. The README identifies the current manuscript and explains the nested evidence, verification, and reconstruction routes.
