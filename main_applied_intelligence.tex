\title{Diagnostic Utility Depends on the Model Portfolio: A Frozen Graph-vs-MLP Decision Benchmark}
\author*[1]{\fnm{Mengdan} \sur{Xue}}\email{17326961775@163.com}
\affil*[1]{\orgdiv{Faculty of Computational Mathematics and Cybernetics}, \orgname{Lomonosov Moscow State University}, \orgaddress{\city{Moscow}, \country{Russia}}}

\abstract{A graph diagnostic guides a model-selection decision whose value depends on the candidate models, their training, and abstention fallback. We evaluate nine fixed graph-versus-multilayer-perceptron (MLP) policies on 11 node-classification datasets and ten seeds. The pre-specified benchmark allocates 24 tuning trials across six graph architectures and four to MLP. The historical homophily/MLP-validation rule, Combined, with MLP fallback incurs 7.46 percentage points of oracle-portfolio regret, versus 0.26 for always choosing graph and 0.22 for validation selection. Four datasets contribute 95.1\% of Combined's loss, and abstaining units contribute 75.9\%; these shares describe the joint outcomes of routing and trained predictions. Post-hoc restrictions favor Combined in three of 63 graph portfolios, comprising a graph convolutional network, a graph attention network, and their pair. Jointly removing Cornell and Wisconsin eliminates those frozen-record advantages. A separate post-hoc retraining under two input parameterizations and an expanded 24-trial MLP search retains Combined's higher full-portfolio regret averaged over all 11 datasets with MLP fallback. With centered-scaled inputs and 24 MLP trials, Combined incurs 5.75 points, against 0.53 for always-graph; a leave-one-dataset-out calibrated adjusted-homophily rule incurs 0.15. Its gain is confined to Cornell and Wisconsin. The result separates fixed heuristics from calibrated diagnostics. Dataset-level primary tests establish no superiority after multiplicity correction. The offline decision audit quantifies losses, coverage, fallback, and improvement over reference policies. The findings locate diagnostic utility within the evaluated portfolios and training configurations, with retained records supporting reconstruction.}

\keywords{Graph neural networks, Model selection, Graph diagnostics, Node classification, Benchmark evaluation, Decision regret}

\subsection*{Data and code availability}
The accompanying archive, \path{applied-intelligence-journal-supplement-2026-10-04.zip}, contains the manuscript source, analysis code, configurations, and four evidence archives for the frozen benchmark and its earlier sensitivity analyses. Three retain their original bytes; the training-repeatability archive is a documented derivative with machine-local paths redacted. The \path{new-evidence/} directory adds all 1,540 input-retraining records and 220 MLP-budget-extension records, including trial histories, saved analyses, checkpoint hash indexes, and completion receipts. The matching executed code is in \path{extension-source/}. The README gives verification and offline reconstruction commands; \path{PACKAGE_MANIFEST.json}, \path{PUBLIC_DERIVATION.json}, and \path{VERSION.json} bind the delivered files, metadata-only transformations, and source revisions. The primary benchmark and each post-hoc record set remain separate. Exact numerical reconstruction is validated on Windows; cross-platform bitwise identity is not claimed. A documented provenance amendment retains the earlier appendix summary and binds its recomputed decision statistics to the included audit. Full model checkpoint tensors, execution ledgers, and Git history remain in author storage, so the supplement supports score reconstruction but not independent checkpoint replay. The original public datasets and loaders are identified in Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}.
