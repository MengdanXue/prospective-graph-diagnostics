\title{Diagnostic Utility Depends on the Model Portfolio: A Frozen Graph-vs-MLP Decision Benchmark}
\author*[1]{\fnm{Mengdan} \sur{Xue}}\email{17326961775@163.com}
\affil*[1]{\orgdiv{Faculty of Computational Mathematics and Cybernetics}, \orgname{Lomonosov Moscow State University}, \orgaddress{\city{Moscow}, \country{Russia}}}

\abstract{Assessing a graph diagnostic requires evaluating the complete model-selection policy, including candidate models and abstention fallback. We audit nine graph-versus-multilayer-perceptron policies on 11 node-classification datasets and ten seeds. The pre-specified benchmark compares a six-architecture graph portfolio with 24 tuning trials against a feature-only multilayer perceptron with four. Under this configuration, the combined diagnostic and its declared fallback incur 7.46 percentage points of oracle-portfolio regret, versus 0.26 for always choosing graph and 0.22 for validation selection. Four datasets account for 95.1\% of the combined policy's loss: two have input-sensitive multilayer-perceptron training, and two use original graph versions with known duplicate-node concerns. Abstaining units, assigned to the multilayer perceptron, account for 75.9\% of recorded loss. Separate paired preprocessing experiments change the magnitude of regret while retaining the graph advantage on the two tested datasets. Post-hoc portfolio restrictions reverse the comparison with always-graph for a graph convolutional network (GCN) or graph attention network (GAT). Only these architectures and their pair favor the rule among 63 candidate subsets; removing Cornell and Wisconsin eliminates those advantages. Dataset-level tests establish no superiority after multiplicity correction. The findings quantify how fixed diagnostic decisions incur loss under evaluated portfolios, fallback choices, and baseline training. Retained records support an offline audit of regret, coverage, loss concentration, and available improvement over reference policies.}

\keywords{Graph neural networks, Model selection, Graph diagnostics, Node classification, Benchmark evaluation, Decision regret}
