\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Diagnostic Utility Is a Property of the Decision Setting}
\label{sec:mismatch}

The portfolio comparison quantifies a familiar architectural distinction at the level of decision loss. Low label homophily can coexist with useful graph information for models that separate feature and structural processing or distinguish neighborhood orders~\citep{zhu2020beyond,lim2021linkx,platonov2023characterizing}. In the retained records, validation selects LINKX or H2GCN in every unit on Roman-empire, Amazon-ratings, and Squirrel, yet Combined assigns all those units to MLP after fallback (Table~\ref{tab:winning_architectures}). Chameleon has mixed selections, including GAT in four units. These records explain why the available architectures matter when a homophily rule is scored; they do not isolate an architectural cause independently of training and dataset composition.

Restricting the graph action to GCN or GAT reverses its mean comparison with the same diagnostic decisions. At equal eight-trial budgets, GCN+GAT and H2GCN+LINKX also give different orderings. The empirical contribution is this comparison of complete policy losses against fixed reference rules, including the circumstances under which the ordering changes.

Available improvement helps explain the contrast. Always-graph has only 0.256 percentage points of regret in the full portfolio, leaving little absolute improvement for any selector using these outcomes. The GCN and GAT portfolios leave more headroom. Absolute regret and improvement over a reference remain separate questions: GPR-GNN gives lower absolute Combined regret than GCN or GAT but favors always-graph in the paired comparison. Each restricted portfolio also defines its own oracle. The observed reversals therefore concern these model-selection settings, with favorable averages concentrated in Cornell and Wisconsin.

The reusable audit takes paired validation-selected model outcomes, diagnostic inputs, and an explicit fallback. It first fixes the available model families, scores complete decisions against always-graph and validation selection, and reports reference headroom; it then locates losses by dataset and action before changing portfolios or fallback. For example, the frozen full portfolio leaves only 0.256 points of improvement over always-graph, while Combined loses 7.457 points and 75.9\% of its loss occurs on abstaining units. This directs attention to routing and the fallback predictions rather than covered-set accuracy alone. Once both families have been tuned, validation selection is a usable reference. Earlier training screening would additionally require measuring input-acquisition and training costs.

\subsection{Fallback and Training Determine the Measured Cost}

The magnitude of the primary loss depends heavily on four datasets: Roman-empire, Amazon-ratings, Squirrel, and Chameleon contribute 95.1\% of Combined's total regret. This concentration makes baseline and data quality central to its interpretation. Input-parameterization diagnostics show MLP training degradation on the first two datasets. The original Squirrel and Chameleon versions have known duplicate-node concerns, while their exclusion sensitivity evaluates the remaining datasets. The concentration alone cannot attribute their losses to those concerns.

Fallback interacts with these baseline outcomes. With the declared MLP fallback, Combined's full-set action reduces to a homophily threshold; its MLP validation threshold determines which choices are recorded as abstentions. The 75.9\% loss share after abstention identifies a group of records. Its size reflects the predictions selected on those units as well as the routing rule. The paired two-dataset preprocessing experiment makes the interaction concrete: all 20 normalized-feature units abstain, while all 20 raw-feature units explicitly select MLP. With MLP fallback, mean regret falls from 20.876 to 13.299 points; graph fallback removes the normalized-condition regret but leaves the raw-condition regret unchanged (Table~\ref{tab:preprocessing_fallback}). These retraining outcomes are separate sensitivity estimates, not substitutions into the original 11-dataset average.

The resulting interpretation is that a full-policy audit must inspect both routing and the trained predictions it routes to. A shared preprocessing transform alone does not ensure an adequately trained feature-only baseline, and search breadth does not substitute for input parameterization: with normalized features, no validation epoch of any of the 24 MLP trials on Amazon-ratings exceeds the majority-class validation proportion, and each selected checkpoint attains the majority-class test proportion. The expanded search leaves Roman-empire far below its centered-scaled accuracy (Section~\ref{sec:mlp_budget}). The degree-preserving intervention separately demonstrates useful edge organization for GCN on three citation networks; it addresses graph use in those models, while the largest selection losses concern different datasets and architectures.

\subsection{Scope of the Evidence}

The simple rules support an inspectable case analysis. A post-hoc, leave-one-dataset-out calibrated evaluation of two published graph statistics appears in Appendix~\ref{app:mlp_budget}. Adjusted homophily attains lower descriptive regret than always-graph under both inputs and both MLP budgets, whereas edge label informativeness varies with the configuration. In CS with 24 MLP trials, its entire mean gain comes from Cornell and Wisconsin, with exact ties on the other nine datasets. This distinguishes fixed-heuristic performance from that of a calibrated policy while retaining the small-graph concentration as a limitation. These calibrated rules use the published statistics rather than reproducing their authors' selectors; learned model selectors remain untested. Thresholds were fixed after legacy aggregate inspection and before the primary records. Subsequent portfolio restrictions, calibration, and influence checks are post-hoc. The dependence of favorable frozen-subset averages on Cornell and Wisconsin limits transport to independent datasets.

In the frozen benchmark, the full graph action receives 24 trials against four for MLP. Single-architecture restrictions match trial counts while changing candidate availability, and they reuse the original outcomes. A post-hoc extension on separately trained records gives the MLP 24 trials (Section~\ref{sec:mlp_budget}); it expands the hyperparameter search of one MLP architecture, while the graph action still chooses among six architectures, so the architecture-level asymmetry of the two portfolios remains. None of these analyses equalizes time or FLOPs, and the extension was designed after inspecting partial results. Thus the full-portfolio comparison measures the specified allocation of architecture and tuning opportunities. The 11-dataset retraining with a training-fitted input and a 24-trial MLP search preserves the direction of the full-portfolio comparison but is not an exact replay of the original records and does not show that every stronger baseline would preserve it; filtered graph versions remain unevaluated.

The operational conclusion rests on descriptive losses. Fixed policy agreement limits the key comparison to at most seven nonzero dataset differences, making rejection unattainable under the realized dataset composition and chosen Holm family. The inferential analysis quantifies this resolution limit; non-rejection supplies no evidence of equivalence.

Finally, reconstruction of decisions from retained outcomes and repeatability of model training are separate properties. The bounded LINKX audit records default-CUDA variability and identical histories within its specified deterministic repeat groups. It characterizes the tested configurations rather than certifying the original benchmark's training. The released snapshot supports record verification; the private predecessor history and large checkpoint tensors remain in the author archive.

Representative published diagnostics evaluated prospectively, and independent datasets, would test whether these decision costs persist when the selector or its baseline changes. Reporting the portfolio, baseline training, fallback, and available headroom makes that comparison interpretable.
