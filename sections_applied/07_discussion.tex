\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Where a Diagnostic Creates Value}
\label{sec:mismatch}

The action-level audit identifies two requirements for a useful selector: its reference policy must leave opportunities for improvement, and its changes of action must capture those opportunities without greater losses elsewhere. In the frozen full portfolio, always-graph leaves only 0.256 percentage points of headroom. Combined incurs 7.457 points of regret, whereas restricting the graph action to GCN or GAT creates substantially more headroom and reverses its descriptive comparison with always-graph. Equal eight-trial graph budgets also produce different orderings for GCN+GAT and H2GCN+LINKX. Candidate composition therefore changes the task that the diagnostic is asked to solve.

This dependence is visible in the selected models. Validation chooses LINKX or H2GCN on every Roman-empire, Amazon-ratings, and Squirrel unit in the frozen benchmark, while Combined resolves all those units to MLP. These architectures can use structural and feature information differently from one-hop aggregation~\citep{zhu2020beyond,lim2021linkx,platonov2023characterizing}. The recorded choices explain where the loss occurs without separating an architectural effect from training and dataset composition. Each portfolio also defines its own oracle: a smaller absolute regret in one row does not imply a larger gain over its reference policy.

Adjusted homophily supplies a useful counterexample to treating the fixed-rule result as a verdict on diagnostics generally. Its calibrated policy has lower descriptive full-portfolio regret than always-graph in the retraining. In CS with 24 MLP trials, it selects MLP on Cornell and Wisconsin and graph elsewhere; all net improvement comes from those two datasets. The matched-calibration analysis makes the threshold-fitting procedure common across the three statistics. It supports comparison of the resulting policies under this procedure, while the concentration of gains limits the evidence for transfer.

\subsection{Routing and Baseline Quality Must Be Audited Together}

An abstention is an incomplete decision until its fallback is specified. Combined demonstrates why this matters: under MLP fallback, its MLP-validation cutoff affects coverage but leaves its full-set action unchanged. The 75.9\% of frozen regret recorded after abstention consequently describes the joint outcomes of routing and the selected predictors. It does not measure an isolated causal effect of abstaining. Reporting only covered-set accuracy would omit those realized costs.

Training quality changes the same accounting. Four datasets account for 95.1\% of Combined's frozen loss. Input diagnostics identify degraded MLP training on Roman-empire and Amazon-ratings; the original Chameleon and Squirrel versions have known duplicate-node concerns. Separate retraining under a training-fitted centered-scaled input and a wider MLP search changes the available outcomes. It retains Combined's higher full-portfolio regret under MLP fallback, but the joint fallback/exclusion analysis finds local exceptions under normalized inputs. The baseline comparison must therefore name the input, candidate search, fallback, and dataset composition together.

Additional hyperparameter search alone does not guarantee an adequate feature-only baseline. Under normalized inputs, none of the 24 MLP trials on Amazon-ratings exceeds the majority-class validation proportion, and each selected checkpoint attains the majority-class test proportion. Roman-empire remains far below its centered-scaled MLP accuracy. These observations justify inspecting optimization and input parameterization before interpreting a large graph--MLP gap. They also explain why the new training records remain separate from the original benchmark rather than replacing unfavorable outcomes retrospectively.

\subsection{What the Audit Establishes and What Remains Open}

The contribution is a connected empirical evaluation of complete policies, with records that expose action changes, benefit, harm, and reference headroom. These accounting identities and information-control principles are established ideas; the evidence here concerns their consequences for the evaluated graph-selection setting. The simple fixed rules make those consequences inspectable. The calibrated statistics broaden the comparison, but neither they nor the rules represent the full range of published or learned graph-model selectors.

The primary and exploratory evidence have different roles. The primary protocol followed inspection of legacy aggregates but preceded generation of its comparative records. Portfolio restrictions, retraining, the MLP extension, and matched calibration are post-hoc. Dataset-level primary tests establish no superiority after multiplicity correction: at most seven nonzero dataset differences make rejection unattainable under the realized Holm family. This is a resolution limit, not evidence of equivalence. The new audit reports descriptive values, without assigning the older four-trial intervals to the 24-trial extension or treating overlapping sensitivity cases as independent experiments.

The 24-trial extension matches trial counts between actions, but one action searches a single MLP architecture and the other searches six graph architectures. Time and FLOPs are not equalized. The convenience sample of eleven datasets, influential small WebKB graphs, shared calibration datasets, and unevaluated filtered graph versions remain limits on external validity. A prospective test on independent datasets with richer diagnostics and a broader feature-only baseline would address these limits.

Finally, exact score reconstruction, repeatable model training, and practical training-cost savings are distinct claims. The retained outcomes support the first on the validated platform. The bounded LINKX audit characterizes repeatability only within its tested configurations. The present offline study trains both actions and measures prediction loss; establishing an efficient early selector additionally requires measuring diagnostic acquisition and avoided training costs. The auxiliary edge intervention shows that graph structure can matter for GCN while answering a different question from policy utility.
