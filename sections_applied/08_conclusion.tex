\section{Conclusion}

The value of a graph diagnostic depends on the complete decision it implements. In the frozen 11-dataset benchmark, Combined with MLP fallback incurs substantially more regret than always-graph for the full six-architecture portfolio. Restricting the graph candidates changes the comparison, and separate retraining shows how input parameterization and MLP search alter its magnitude. Joint fallback/exclusion checks delimit the configurations in which that ordering persists.

Calibrated adjusted homophily provides a positive descriptive result under the retraining configurations, with its CS/24 advantage confined to Cornell and Wisconsin. Matching the threshold-calibration procedure across statistics and tracing benefits and harms to effective actions makes both favorable and unfavorable results interpretable. The resulting audit specifies the candidate portfolio, trained outcomes, fallback, available improvement, and reconstruction boundary. Its retained records support checking those claims; broader diagnostic coverage and independent datasets are needed to establish how far the observed utility transfers.
