\section{Conclusion}

The frozen 11-dataset benchmark locates the losses of simple graph-selection rules within a specified model portfolio and training protocol. Combined and its declared MLP fallback incur greater regret than always-graph with the full six-architecture portfolio. Four datasets account for 95.1\% of that loss, linking its interpretation to observed baseline degradation and unresolved data-version sensitivity. Loss after abstention is part of this joint evaluation of routing and trained predictions. Post-hoc GCN and GAT restrictions of the frozen records reverse the descriptive ordering, with their advantages depending on Cornell and Wisconsin. In a post-hoc retraining of all 11 datasets under two inputs, expanding the MLP search from four to 24 trials changed most selected MLP configurations and reduced the regret of the fixed heuristics that can choose MLP, but did not reverse the full-portfolio comparison with always-graph averaged over all 11 datasets with MLP fallback; the graph action still chooses among six architectures, and the effect of the input on the feature-only baseline was strongly dataset-dependent.

The calibrated adjusted-homophily rule attains lower descriptive regret than always-graph in the retraining, with its CS/24 gain confined to Cornell and Wisconsin. The negative full-portfolio result therefore concerns the evaluated fixed heuristics rather than all diagnostics.

The practical contribution is an offline audit that combines established evaluation principles to locate prediction losses and explain their dependence on the decision setting. Benchmark builders and applied researchers can assess full-set regret, fallback, and coverage against references appropriate to the information available, including validation selection after tuning. The retained records support reconstruction of the reported scores, while the sensitivity analyses delimit their interpretation. Applying this audit to richer diagnostics and independent datasets would test the reach of the observed dependencies.
