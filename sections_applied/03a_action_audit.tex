\subsection{An Action-Level Audit of Diagnostic Utility}
\label{sec:action_audit}

To compare diagnostics, we first resolve abstentions into effective actions. Let $q_u\in\{\mathrm{G},\mathrm{M}\}$ be that action and let $\mathbb{E}_D$ average seeds within each dataset and then weight datasets equally. Write $g_u=a_u^{\mathrm{M}}-a_u^{\mathrm{G}}$ and $x_+=\max(x,0)$. The improvement over always-graph satisfies the accounting identity
\begin{align}
R_{\mathrm{G}}-R_q
 &= \mathbb{E}_D[\mathbf{1}\{q_u=\mathrm{M}\}g_u] \\
 &= \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_D[\mathbf{1}\{q_u=\mathrm{M}\}(g_u)_+]}_{B_q:\ \text{benefit}}
 -\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_D[\mathbf{1}\{q_u=\mathrm{M}\}(-g_u)_+]}_{H_q:\ \text{harm}}.
\label{eq:benefit_harm}
\end{align}
It follows directly by subtracting the two regrets in Eq.~\eqref{eq:oracle_regret}. Only units on which the policy chooses MLP contribute to the comparison. The identity separates correctly exploited opportunities from losses incurred by switching away from graph; it supplies an audit of recorded decisions, without a causal attribution to the statistic or architecture.

The available improvement over always-graph is bounded by its own regret,
\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{G}}-R_q\leq R_{\mathrm{G}}=\mathbb{E}_D[(g_u)_+].
\label{eq:headroom_bound}
\end{equation}
We report this headroom in absolute accuracy points. A small ceiling limits the possible absolute gain but does not establish that a diagnostic is uninformative. Dividing a gain by headroom can describe its relative size when the denominator is positive; it cannot increase the absolute benefit or establish statistical significance. The ceiling is conditional on the recorded candidate outcomes and changes when the portfolio or training changes.

Two rules with identical effective actions have identical full-set regret even if their scores, confidence rankings, or abstention coverage differ. Combined supplies a concrete example: under MLP fallback, changing its MLP-validation cutoff changes the explicit-action/abstention labels without changing its effective homophily-threshold action. Conversely, changing fallback is consequential only on abstaining units. These observations motivate reporting scores, explicit actions, fallback-resolved actions, and outcome losses as separate parts of the decision record.

\subsection{What Saved Outcomes Can Reconstruct}
\label{sec:replay_scope}

The retained records support counterfactual choices among already evaluated predictors. A portfolio restriction can reapply the fixed validation rule to retained architecture winners, and a new threshold or fallback can select between their saved outcomes. These operations preserve model training, candidate availability, and the recorded within-architecture selection. A change to preprocessing, training, hyperparameter generation, or any upstream selection that changes the available predictors requires new model records. In particular, the 24-trial MLP extension includes 20 additional trials per unit and a selected-checkpoint evaluation; it is not inferred from the original four-trial outcomes.

Threshold calibration has a further information boundary. Selected-model test outcomes from the other ten datasets serve as meta-training targets; neither the held-out dataset's scores nor its outcomes may select the cutoff. Reusing these datasets across folds supports an internal cross-dataset evaluation. Reconstructing that evaluation exactly does not make it prospective or establish transfer to an independent graph collection. The later matched-calibration analysis fixes its reporting scope in code before execution and remains post-hoc relative to the inspected benchmark.
