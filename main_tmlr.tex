\begin{abstract}
Simple graph statistics can guide model selection only insofar as their decisions are useful for the candidate models. We evaluate graph-versus-MLP policies under a frozen protocol covering 11 datasets, ten seeds, seven architectures, and four trials per architecture. The graph action searches six architectures; MLP receives four trials. Label-dependent graph statistics use training labels only, and validation selects configurations and architectures. The combined diagnostic incurs 7.46 percentage points of full-set oracle-portfolio regret, compared with 0.26 for always-graph and 0.22 for validation selection. Post-hoc single-architecture restrictions retain the diagnostic's decisions but change its relative performance: Combined has lower descriptive mean regret than always-graph for GCN (1.74 versus 5.29) and GAT (2.08 versus 6.35), and higher regret for the other four architectures. Enumeration of all 63 candidate subsets and dataset exclusions delimit this observation; removing Cornell and Wisconsin eliminates the favorable ordering. Separate raw-feature retraining on two datasets substantially improves MLP performance, while the graph action remains preferred in those recorded comparisons. Validation-only follow-up diagnoses input-parameterization sensitivity and exposes graph-training variability. The findings therefore concern the evaluated models and training protocol, not universal diagnostic failure, equal-compute effects, or established generalization. Dataset-level inference does not establish superiority after the predeclared multiplicity correction; non-significance is not equivalence. We provide frozen records and a decision-evaluation protocol linking information boundaries, selection, abstention, regret, and dataset-level uncertainty.
\end{abstract}
