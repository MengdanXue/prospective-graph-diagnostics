\begin{abstract}
Simple graph statistics are often proposed as inexpensive guides to model selection, but their decision value must be evaluated against the models they select. We study graph-versus-MLP selection under a frozen protocol covering 11 datasets, ten seeds, seven architectures, and four tuning trials per architecture. Label-dependent graph statistics use training labels only, and validation selects configurations and architectures. The combined diagnostic attains 55.5\% selection accuracy at 68.2\% coverage and incurs 7.46 percentage points of full-set regret, compared with 0.26 for always-graph and 0.22 for validation selection. A post-hoc single-architecture sensitivity analysis changes the graph action while retaining the diagnostic's decisions and the same four-trial grid. The combined rule has lower descriptive mean regret than always-graph for GCN (1.74 versus 5.29 points) and GAT (2.08 versus 6.35), but higher regret for LINKX (7.99 versus 2.13). The other three restrictions also favor always-graph on mean regret. These comparisons match trial counts, not training time or FLOPs, and do not establish an architecture-independent ordering or out-of-dataset generalization. They show that the observed value of a fixed diagnostic changes with its candidate portfolio. Dataset-level inference limits confirmatory conclusions; non-significance is not evidence of equivalence. A separate degree-preserving intervention reduces GCN accuracy on three citation networks, distinguishing useful edge organization from reliable diagnostic decisions. We provide immutable unit-level artifacts and a reproducible evaluation protocol linking information boundaries, selection, abstention, regret, and dataset-level inference.
\end{abstract}
