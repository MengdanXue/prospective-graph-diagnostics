\begin{abstract}
Choosing whether to use graph structure is an operational decision, yet graph statistics are usually evaluated as correlates of performance rather than as decision rules. We test whether transparent diagnostics whose label-dependent graph statistics use training labels only can select between tuned graph and feature-only multilayer perceptron (MLP) portfolios for node classification, and we explain why they cannot. Before generating confirmatory records, we freeze datasets, splits, seeds, seven architectures, four-trial grids, the practical margin, label scope, abstention, and analysis; the final benchmark contains 11 datasets, 110 dataset--seed units, and 770 model records with no failed units. The combined diagnostic attains 55.5\% selection accuracy, 68.2\% coverage, and 7.46 percentage points of full-set regret, against 80.9\% and 0.26 points for always-graph and 91.8\% and 0.22 points for validation selection. The failure is structural rather than a matter of threshold tuning: the statistic and the decision target are anti-correlated over the regime that dominates the loss. Heterophily-aware architectures win 95 of the 110 units, including every unit on the two most homophilous datasets, so the graph action is not a proxy for GCN-style message passing; meanwhile the four datasets carrying 95.1\% of the combined rule's regret all fall below the frozen homophily threshold, where the rule declines the graph action on all 40 units and 35 of them are won by a heterophily-aware architecture. No threshold on a scalar one-hop summary repairs this, and the diagnosis extends to any such rule paired with a heterophily-aware portfolio. An equal-total-compute check, recomputed from the same frozen records without retraining, confirms the account and bounds the claim: restricting the graph action to one architecture lowers the combined rule's mean regret to 1.74 points against a GCN-only action and 2.08 against GAT, while raising it to 7.99 against LINKX. Diagnostic utility is thus a property of the pairing between statistic and portfolio, and the dataset-level direction remains inconsistent at matched budget, so the diagnostics provide no stable incremental value---not no value under every portfolio. A separate degree-preserving randomization reduces GCN accuracy by 45.5, 29.9, and 17.3 points on Cora, CiteSeer, and PubMed, so the failure is not evidence that edge organization is uninformative. Alongside these findings we deliver the evaluation protocol itself---frozen decision-time information boundaries, matched trivial and validation-based policies, abstention and coverage, oracle-portfolio regret, test-once accounting, dataset-level inference, immutable per-unit provenance, and a deterministic audit binding every reported value to the frozen artifacts---so that richer or learned selectors can be tested on the same terms.
\end{abstract}
