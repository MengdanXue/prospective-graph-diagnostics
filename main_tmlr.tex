\begin{abstract}
We evaluate the measured decision utility of fixed graph-versus-MLP policies on 11 datasets and ten seeds under a frozen protocol. Label-dependent statistics use training labels only, and validation selects models. The graph action searches six architectures with four trials each; MLP receives four trials. The combined rule with its declared MLP fallback incurs 7.46 percentage points of full-set oracle-portfolio regret, compared with 0.26 for always-graph and 0.22 for validation selection; 75.9\% of its regret occurs on abstaining units. Post-hoc restrictions of the evaluated portfolios reverse its relative performance: with unchanged diagnostic decisions, Combined has lower descriptive mean regret than always-graph for GCN (1.74 versus 5.29) and GAT (2.08 versus 6.35), but higher regret for the other four architectures. Across all 63 candidate subsets, only GCN, GAT, and their pair favor Combined; removing Cornell and Wisconsin eliminates those advantages. Two-dataset preprocessing and validation-only training diagnostics identify sensitivity to the tested training configurations. The dataset-level tests establish no superiority after multiplicity correction; structural policy agreement makes rejection unattainable for the key comparison under the realized dataset composition and fixed comparison family. The findings describe measured diagnostic utility within the evaluated protocol, without establishing external performance or equal-compute effects. We provide frozen records and a decision-evaluation protocol that makes candidate models, information boundaries, abstention, regret, and dataset-level uncertainty explicit.
\end{abstract}
